3 The two clocks: MTTD and MTTR

Every incident runs two stopwatches back to back: MTTD (compromise → you detect them) and MTTR (detect → you evict them). Added together they are the total incident lifecycle. Drag the detection speed and watch the total — and which clock dominates it — change:

MTTR is held at a fixed response-capability floor — in reality it grows when the attacker has entrenched longer.

One precise definition
Dwell time ≈ MTTD = compromise → detection — the stretch the attacker sits inside undetected. Then MTTD + MTTR = compromise → fully evicted = the total incident lifecycle. So dwell time is only the first clock; the total is both. Shrinking dwell is what limits how much an attacker can steal — but note MTTR is overloaded across the industry (Respond / Resolve / Recover / Repair), so here it always means detection → evicted.
Remember

In the cloud, Identify is undermined by asset sprawl (ephemeral containers, Shadow IT) and Protect by configuration complexity (the open S3 bucket) — so prevention eventually fails and the goal pivots to detection: find them fast. The stack is logs → SIEM (aggregate, store, and correlate) → XDR (AI that links signals across layers, e.g. impossible travel) → SOC analyst (the human who hunts). Success is measured by two clocks: MTTD (time to detect) and MTTR (time to remediate). Dwell time — the undetected stretch — is the compromise-to-detection window (MTTD); shrink it from months to minutes.
