Buyer, not Builder: the SaaS pivot

Module 12 SaaS pivot Shared responsibility
In this note
  • The pivot from builder (IaaS) to buyer (SaaS) — run your own Exchange vs. subscribe to Office 365
  • What changes: the vendor takes the network, host, and app — the security stack you secure shrinks
  • The two layers that never transfer: identity and data — one note on each

1 The pivot: builder → buyer

For most of this course you were a builder — in IaaS you spin up servers, patch them, configure firewalls. SaaS makes you a buyer: you just consume the app. Same goal (company email), wildly different effort — toggle:

Running email: build it vs. buy it

2 What changes: only identity & data stay yours

As you move from IaaS to SaaS, the stack you secure shrinks dramatically. The controls you built in earlier modules become the vendor’s job. Toggle to see what falls away:

What you still secure

You secure Vendor secures

The controls that drop off your plate
Everything you built as a defender now belongs to the vendor: the network firewall & DMZ (Module 5), NACLs and security groups, host antivirus / EDR and patching (Module 6), even the application code (Module 11). You stop configuring all of it.
Identity is the new perimeter
With no firewall or DMZ around a SaaS app on the public internet, often the only thing between an attacker and your data is a login screen — so identity becomes the perimeter, and MFA matters more than ever. What’s always yours: identity (the people) and data (the information).
